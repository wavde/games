# Games in detail

The per-game reference: difficulty levels, how boards are generated and checked, and how each AI plays. The [README](../README.md) has the overview.

## Generators & difficulty

Logic puzzles generate uniquely-solvable boards in the browser (norinori and lits use an offline-built bank):

| Game | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| lights-out | 3 random taps | 6 taps | 10 taps |
| connect-four (AI) | depth 2 | depth 4 | depth 6 |
| reversi (AI) | depth 1 | depth 3 | depth 5 |
| dots-and-boxes (AI) | greedy + random | + safe lines, fewest-box sacrifice | + endgame search, chain analysis, double-dealing |
| gomoku (AI) | random among top 4 | best heuristic move | 4-ply alpha-beta, 8 candidates |
| checkers (AI) | depth 2, random among near-best | depth 4 | depth 8 |
| morris (AI) | 2 plies, random among near-best | 4 plies | deepest in 0.8 s |
| hex (AI) | 150 random fills, top 5 | 2,500 fills | fills for 1.2 s |
| mancala (AI) | depth 2, random among near-best | depth 6 | deepest in 0.8 s |
| battleships (AI) | random hunt, then neighbours of hits | checkerboard hunt, follows lines of hits | probability heat-map over every placement |
| mini-sudoku | 22 clues | 18 clues | 14 clues |
| tango | 16 givens, 10 edges | 10 givens, 8 edges | 6 givens, 7 edges |
| queens | 6×6 | 7×7 | 8×8 |
| zip | 5×5, ~6 numbers | 6×6, ~8 numbers, ~2 walls | 7×7, ~11 numbers, ~3–4 walls |
| patches | 5×5 partition | 6×6 partition | 7×7 partition |
| nonogram | 5×5 | 8×8 | 10×10 |
| hashi | 7×7, ~9 islands, counting rules | 9×9, ~15, look-ahead | 11×11, ~22, deeper look-ahead |
| akari | 7×7, direct rules | 9×9, look-ahead | 11×11, fewer clues, look-ahead |
| slitherlink | 6×6, direct rules | 7×7, look-ahead | 8×8, fewer clues, deeper look-ahead |
| star-battle | 8×8, one unit at a time | 9×9, look-ahead | 10×10, look-ahead |
| kakuro | 6×6, runs ≤ 4, sums alone | 7×7, runs ≤ 5, look-ahead | 8×8, runs ≤ 5, look-ahead |
| hitori | 6×6, opening patterns + direct rules | 8×8, look-ahead | 10×10, look-ahead |
| nurikabe | 6×6, direct rules | 8×8, look-ahead | 10×10, look-ahead |
| calcudoku | 4×4, cages + lines | 5×5, look-ahead | 6×6, cages up to 4, look-ahead |
| skyscrapers | 4×4, a line at a time | 5×5, look-ahead | 6×6, fewest clues that stay unique |
| futoshiki | 5×5, direct deduction | 6×6, look-ahead | 7×7, fewest clues that stay unique |
| tents | 6×6, direct rules | 8×8, look-ahead | 10×10, never direct-rules-only |
| jigsaw-sudoku | 6×6, singles | 8×8, look-ahead | 9×9, fewest givens that stay unique |
| norinori | 6×6, direct rules | 7×7, look-ahead | 8×8, never direct-rules-only |
| lits | 6×6, direct rules | 8×8, look-ahead | 9×9, never direct-rules-only |
| sokoban | 7×7 room, 2 boxes (par ~10) | 8×8, 3 boxes (par ~18) | 9×9, 4 boxes (par ~27) |
| code-breaker | 4 pegs, 6 colors, 10 guesses | 4 / 6 / 8 | 5 / 8 / 10 |
| slide-15 | 3×3 | 4×4 | 5×5 |

Logic-puzzle generators check uniqueness against a solver before returning (nonogram requires a full line-by-line solve; hashi, akari, slitherlink, star-battle, kakuro, hitori, nurikabe, calcudoku, skyscrapers, futoshiki, tents, jigsaw-sudoku, norinori, and lits are graded by how much look-ahead they need). zip adds numbers and walls until every other path is ruled out. norinori and lits boards take seconds to make unique, so they come from a bank of 150 per level built offline by `.github/tools/` and re-checked in CI. slide-15 scrambles by legal moves, so it is always solvable; sokoban rooms are searched backwards from the solved position, so they are always solvable and the start chosen is the one needing the most pushes.

## Engines and AIs

- **Rendering**: HTML + CSS Grid; snake and breakout draw on `<canvas>`.
- **State**: plain JS, no framework.
- **AI**:
  - *tic-tac-toe*: full-tree minimax (unbeatable).
  - *chess*: alpha-beta + MVV-LVA move ordering + piece-square tables.
  - *connect-four*: alpha-beta with center-first column ordering.
  - *reversi*: negamax alpha-beta, corner-first ordering, positional weights + mobility.
  - *gomoku*: scores every 5-cell window for attack and defence; always takes a win or blocks an immediate five; hard adds 4-ply alpha-beta over the best 8 candidates.
  - *checkers*: negamax alpha-beta that always follows capture sequences to the end, with iterative deepening under a 0.8 s budget; material, advancement, back-row and centre terms, trades down when ahead, and drives kings at the enemy in the endgame. Two kings v one king is solved exactly (built on first use), so won king endings are converted. Rules verified against published perft counts to depth 9.
  - *mancala*: negamax alpha-beta where an extra turn is the same player moving again; extra-turn and capture moves searched first; store and on-side seed difference; hard deepens until its 0.8 s budget runs out. Rules checked against an independent Kalah implementation over 5,000 random games.
  - *battleships*: easy hunts at random and tries the squares around a hit; medium hunts on a checkerboard sized to the smallest unsunk ship and follows lines of hits; hard sums every placement of every unsunk ship consistent with the shots so far (placements through open hits weighted heavily) and fires at the hottest square. Sinking a random fleet takes ~63 / 51 / 45 shots on average.
  - *morris*: negamax alpha-beta with a transposition table, mill-closing moves searched past the horizon, and even-depth iterative deepening; material, open twos, and mobility.
  - *hex*: Monte-Carlo with all-moves-as-first scoring — fills the rest of the board at random and favours cells it owned in the fills it won; always takes a win and blocks an immediate loss.
  - *dots-and-boxes*: takes free boxes and avoids third sides; hard searches the last safe moves, values the chain/loop endgame exactly, and double-deals when keeping control pays.
- **Klondike deals**: before a deal is used, a depth-first solver that can see the face-down cards must find a win (safe foundation plays automatic, moves pruned to ones that turn a card, empty a column, or free a card for its foundation, seen positions skipped). Unproven deals are skipped; about 60% of draw-1 and 50% of draw-3 deals pass within the budget.
- **FreeCell deals**: Microsoft's LCG shuffle, so deal numbers match the classic Windows set (checked against the published layouts of deals #1 and #617); #11982, the one unwinnable deal, is skipped.
- **PRNG**: mulberry32 seeded by FNV-1a hash of date+difficulty+game — deterministic dailies across devices.

## Build notes

How each game was built: board sizes, generators, solvers, and what CI checks.

| Game | Notes |
|---|---|
| **code-breaker** | Mastermind-style; 4/6/10, 4/6/8, 5/8/10 (pegs/colors/guesses); duplicate-safe scoring; best = fewest guesses. |
| **slide-15** | 3×3/4×4/5×5; scramble is a random walk of legal slides (always solvable); line slides, arrows, undo; best = fewest moves. |
| **nonogram** | 5×5/8×8/10×10; generator keeps only boards a line-by-line solver fully determines (unique, no guessing); fill/mark tools, straight-line drag, right-click mark; best time. |
| **reversi** | Othello rules incl. passes; vs. AI (negamax alpha-beta, depth 1/3/5, corner-first ordering, positional + mobility eval) or local 2P; undo to your turn; W–L–D record per level (finished AI games can't be undone). |
| **dots-and-boxes** | 3×3/4×4/5×5 boxes; vs. AI or local 2P. Easy: greedy + random; medium: never opens a box while a safe line exists, sacrifices fewest boxes; hard: + bounded search over the last ≤12 safe lines and exact chain/loop endgame values (opens the cheapest component, double-deals when control pays) — beat medium 19–10–1 on 4×4 and 10–6 on 5×5 in headless AI-vs-AI runs. W–L–D per level and size. |
| **hashi** (bridges) | 7×7/9×9/11×11; grows a connected island network, keeps it only if unique. Graded: easy = counting rules alone, medium = needs look-ahead deduction, hard = larger and not solvable by counting alone. Tap-tap, drag, or tap the gap; crossings blocked. |
| **akari** (light up) | 7×7/9×9/11×11, symmetric walls; starts fully clued and hides numbers while the grade holds. Easy = direct rules alone, medium/hard = need look-ahead. Tap cycles bulb/dot; clashes and satisfied clues shown. |
| **gomoku** | 15×15 freestyle five-in-a-row; vs. AI or local 2P. Window-count heuristic (attack + defence), always wins/blocks fives; hard = 4-ply alpha-beta over 8 candidates (beat medium 5–1–2; ≤110 ms/move). W–L–D per level. |
| **checkers** | 8×8 English draughts: compulsory captures, multi-jumps, crowning ends the move, 40-move no-progress draw; vs. AI or local 2P (board flips when you play white). Negamax alpha-beta at depth 2/4/8 with capture quiescence, iterative deepening, and an 0.8 s think budget (medium beat easy 16–0–4, hard beat medium 20–0–0). Two kings v one king (wins take up to 33 plies) is solved exactly by retrograde analysis on first use (~170 ms, 29,760 entries, verified against an independent recomputation); with it, hard converted 20/20 each of 2K v 1K, 3K v 2K, 3K v 1K, 2K v 1M, and 2K+1M v 1K from random starts. Move generator matches published perft to depth 9 and an independent generator on 20k random positions. W–L–D per level. |
| **slitherlink** | 6×6/7×7/8×8 loop puzzle. Random hole-free region → its outline is the loop; starts fully clued and blanks numbers while the grade holds (easy = direct rules, medium = single-edge look-ahead, hard = unique by search, never direct-rules-solvable). 120/120 sampled boards confirmed unique by an independent OR-Tools CP-SAT model; hard generates in ~150 ms median. Tap cycles line/×; over-full numbers and branching lines turn red. |
| **mancala** | Kalah(6,4): sowing skips the opponent's store, extra turn on landing in your store, captures from your own empty pit, remaining seeds banked when a side empties. vs. AI or local 2P; negamax alpha-beta where an extra turn is the same player moving again, extra-turn/capture moves ordered first; easy = depth 2 with near-best randomness, medium = depth 6, hard = iterative deepening under 0.8 s (medium beat easy 27–3, hard beat medium 19–0–1). Rules match an independent implementation over 5,000 random games (219k moves). Sowing animates seed by seed (skipped under reduced motion); undo to your turn; W–L–D per level. |
| **battleships** | 10×10, classic fleet (5/4/3/3/2), straight non-overlapping ships that may touch; sunk ships revealed. vs. AI: easy = random hunt + neighbours of hits, medium = checkerboard hunt sized to the smallest unsunk ship + follows lines of hits, hard = probability heat-map over every consistent placement. Mean shots to sink a random fleet 63 / 51 / 45 (400 games each); medium beat easy 296–104 and hard beat medium 294–106 head to head. Fleet shuffle until your first shot; W–L and fewest shots to win per level. |
| **star-battle** | Two stars per row/column/region, no touching; 8×8/9×9/10×10. Random valid star layout → nearby stars paired and joined into regions → border cells shifted between regions until a solver finds no rival solution. Graded with the standard human techniques (unit counting, per-unit placement enumeration, region-confinement across consecutive rows/columns): easy = solvable by those alone, medium/hard = need single-cell look-ahead. 120/120 sampled boards confirmed unique (with the intended answer) by an independent OR-Tools CP-SAT model; hard generates in ~150 ms median (≤0.7 s). |
| **kakuro** | Cross sums on 6×6/7×7/8×8 (plus the clue row/column). Symmetric layout repaired so no run is 1 long or longer than the level's cap (4/5/5); random repeat-free fill; then, while a solver finds a rival answer, one differing cell gets the digit whose two run sums allow the fewest digit sets. Solver uses per-run digit-set filtering and required-digit placement; easy = solvable by that alone, medium/hard = need single-digit look-ahead. 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique (with the intended answer) by an independent OR-Tools CP-SAT model; generation ~7/25/32 ms median, ≤0.5 s. Number pad + typing, pencil notes, repeated digits and overshooting clues in red. |
| **hitori** | 6×6/8×8/10×10. Random shading (no shaded neighbours, unshaded cells connected) over a random Latin square; each shaded cell copies a number from its row or column, so the unshaded numbers are distinct by construction. Solver: shaded → neighbours unshaded, unshaded → same-number cells in its lines shaded, connectivity (cells whose shading would split the unshaded area stay unshaded); lone numbers are kept unshaded and uniqueness then checks none of them could be shaded instead. Graded with the opening patterns (sandwich X Y X, adjacent pair) + direct rules for easy, single-cell look-ahead for medium/hard. 120/120 sampled boards confirmed unique by an independent CP-SAT model with lazy connectivity cuts; generation ≤30 ms. |
| **nurikabe** | 6×6/8×8/10×10. Starts all sea and, while a 2×2 pool remains, turns one of its cells to land (joining the one island it touches — preferred, so islands aren't all tiny — or starting a new one, islands kept apart and capped at 5/7/9 cells, sea kept connected); one random cell per island gets its size. Solver: finished islands walled with sea, cells that would merge numbered islands or overfill one are sea, 2×2 pools, reach (cells no unfinished island can still reach are sea; an island with one way out takes it), sea connectivity with cut cells. Easy = those rules alone, medium/hard = single-cell look-ahead. 120/120 sampled boards confirmed unique by an independent CP-SAT model (one-hot island membership, lazy island/sea connectivity cuts); generation ~2/12/110 ms median, hard ≤1.4 s. |
| **sokoban** | Generated rooms (7×7/8×8/9×9 with a wall border and scattered inner walls, floor connected; 2/3/4 boxes). Breadth-first search *backwards* from the solved position (pulling boxes, numeric state keys, capped at 20k/40k/60k states) gives every reachable start with its exact minimum push count; the deepest start of the best of several rooms is used, and its depth is shown as par. Par ~10/18/27 pushes; every sampled level's par matched an independent forward push search; generation ~1/20/400 ms median, ≤0.75 s. Arrow keys/WASD, swipe, tap to push or walk; undo per step or walk; time + best time per level. |
| **calcudoku** | 4×4/5×5/6×6 Latin square in random cages (up to 3/3/4 cells, surplus single cells folded into neighbours); each cage gets ÷/−/+/× (÷ only when exact, 3+ cells + or ×). Solver filters each cage to the digit tuples that still fit, plus row/column singles and hidden singles; boards kept only when a search finds exactly one answer. Easy = those rules alone, medium/hard = single-digit look-ahead. 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique by an independent CP-SAT model; generation ≤10 ms. |
| **skyscrapers** | 4×4/5×5/6×6 Latin square of heights with edge visibility clues. Solver keeps, per row and column, only the orderings of 1..n that fit its two clues and the current domains (full line consistency, ≤720 orderings); probing for medium. Starts with all 4n clues and hides them one at a time while the level holds (easy: line logic alone; medium: look-ahead; hard: still unique, never line-logic-only). 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique by an independent CP-SAT model; generation ≤130 ms. |
| **futoshiki** | 5×5/6×6/7×7 Latin square with < / > signs between neighbours. Solver: singles, hidden singles, and inequality bounds (a cell below a neighbour stays under that neighbour's largest candidate, and vice versa); probing for medium. Starts with every given and every sign, hides givens then signs while the level holds (easy: direct deduction; medium: look-ahead; hard: still unique, never direct-deduction-only); boards end with 0–8 givens. 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique with the intended answer by an independent CP-SAT model; generation ≤160 ms. Screen readers hear each cell's signs ("bigger than below"). |
| **tents** | 6×6/8×8/10×10. Tents scattered so none touch (8-neighbourhood), each with a tree on a free side, until a fifth of the field is tents; layouts kept only when they grade right. Solver: tents turn their eight neighbours to grass, row/column counts fill or clear a line, a tree with one open side gets its tent there, and a bipartite matching (Kuhn) checks every tree can still get its own tent and every tent its own tree. Easy = those rules alone, medium = single-cell look-ahead, hard = unique and never direct-rules-only. 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique with the intended answer by an independent CP-SAT model (explicit tree–tent assignment); generation ≤5 ms. Tap cycles tent/grass, right-click or X marks grass. |
| **klondike** | Draw 1 / draw 3, unlimited redeals, auto-flip, auto-finish once the stock is gone and every card is up. A deal is used only if a depth-first solver that sees the face-down cards wins it (safe foundation plays automatic; moves pruned to whole runs that turn a card or empty a column, part-runs that free a card for its foundation, waste plays, and draws; seen positions skipped; 6,000-node budget per deal, next deal tried otherwise). 400/400 sampled deals' winning lines replayed as legal moves; deal time ≤0.5 s. Tap auto-moves (foundation first, then the next column that takes it), drag for a specific spot, keyboard X picks up and Enter places. Undo to the start; best time per mode. |
| **jigsaw-sudoku** | 6×6/8×8/9×9 with n irregular regions of n cells. Regions start as the rows and keep trading one cell each way across a border while both stay in one piece; a random fill (propagation + shuffled search) gives the answer; givens are removed while the level holds (easy: singles; medium: look-ahead; hard: still unique, never singles-only). Uses the shared digit-puzzle solver. 90/90 sampled boards confirmed unique with the intended answer by an independent CP-SAT model; generation ≤0.7 s. Regions tinted with up to six colours so neighbours differ. |
| **freecell** | Microsoft deal numbers 1–32000 (LCG shuffle verified against deals #1 and #617; #11982 skipped). Multi-card moves limited to (free cells + 1) × 2^(empty columns), halved for an empty destination; safe cards go home automatically. Tap: foundation, then a column that takes it, an empty column, a free cell; drag or keyboard pick-up/place. A best-first test solver won deals #1 and #617 and both were replayed through the UI with taps, drags, and keys. Undo to the start; one daily deal. |
| **morris** | Nine men's morris: placing, sliding, flying at three pieces; a mill takes a piece not in a mill (any piece if all are); loss at two pieces or no move; draw after 25 moves each without a capture. vs. AI or local 2P. Negamax alpha-beta with a transposition table, mill quiescence, even-depth iterative deepening; easy = depth 2 with near-best randomness, medium = depth 4, hard = 0.8 s (≈ depth 6–10). Medium beat easy 6–3–1, hard beat medium 6–0–4. A root-search bug (false ties from bounded scores) was found by comparing against exhaustive minimax and fixed before release. W–L–D per level. |
| **hex** | 7×7/9×9/11×11, Red joins top–bottom, Blue left–right. AI: all-moves-as-first Monte-Carlo over random board fills (exactly one winner on any full board, checked on 2,000 random boards), plus immediate win/block; easy 150 fills top-5, medium 2,500 fills, hard 1.2 s. Medium beat easy 10–0 and hard beat medium 10–0 on 9×9 with colours alternating. Undo to your turn; W–L per level and size. |
| **spider** | Two decks, ten columns (6,6,6,6 then 5s), 50-card stock dealt a row at a time and only when no column is empty. Build down in any suit; only one-suit runs move together; a finished king-to-ace run of one suit leaves the table; eight wins. 1 suit (all spades), 2 (spades and hearts), or 4. Deals aren't checked for a win. Tap (same suit one higher, then any one higher, then an empty column), drag, or keyboard pick-up/place; D deals. Undo to the start; best time per suit count. Random play through the UI (taps, drags, keys, deals) kept all 104 cards in every mode. |
| **norinori** | 6×6/7×7/8×8. Boards come from a bank (150 per level) built offline by `.github/tools/norinori.mjs`: non-touching dominoes packed at random, each shaded cell seeds an area, touching areas paired into regions of two shaded cells, then while a search finds a rival shading a cell it shades moves to another region (cut-off pieces join neighbours). Solver: region counts, domino partner rules, and per-region enumeration of the ways to finish it. Easy = those rules alone, medium = look-ahead, hard = unique and never rules-only. In-page generation was tried first and took seconds per board. All 450 boards confirmed unique with the intended answer by an independent CP-SAT model; CI re-checks every board with the tool's solver. |
| **lits** | 6×6/8×8/9×9. Bank of 150 per level from `.github/tools/lits.mjs`: a connected chain of tetrominoes (no 2×2, matching shapes never touching) grown at random, each one the core of a region, unshaded cells joining neighbours, then the same rival-killing region repair as norinori. Solver: per-region placement lists filtered by known cells, 2×2 blocks, fixed same-shape neighbours, and reachability of the shading. 10×10 hard was tried and dropped (about 14 CPU-minutes per board). All 450 boards confirmed unique with the intended answer by an independent CP-SAT model (placement choice, lazy connectivity cuts); CI re-checks every board. Finished tetrominoes are coloured by shape. |
| **snake** | First real-time game. Canvas (sized for device pixel ratio), fixed-step loop on requestAnimationFrame. Easy 15×15 wrap-around at 170 ms/step; medium 17×17 walls, 125 ms, −1 ms per bite; hard 19×19 walls, 105 ms, −2 ms per bite (floor 60 ms). Rules are DOM-free (`createGame`/`step`): moving into the tail's cell is legal when not growing, reversals are ignored, up to two turns are queued per tick. Arrows/WASD, swipe, tap to pause, on-screen pad; auto-pause on tab switch; best score per speed. |
| **breakout** | Canvas arena 360×480 (logical units, scaled for DPR). 4/5/6 rows × 8 bricks, higher rows worth more; paddle 84/68/54 wide; ball 250/320/390 units/s, ×1.08 per level; 5/3/3 lives. Ball moves in sub-steps no longer than its radius (no tunnelling); paddle hit offset sets the angle up to ±60° from vertical so the ball never goes flat; bricks bounce off the shallower overlap axis. Rules are DOM-free (`createGame`/`update`/`launch`) and CI-checked. Mouse/touch steer + tap to launch, arrows/A-D + Space, hold-to-move buttons; auto-pause on tab switch; best score per speed. |
